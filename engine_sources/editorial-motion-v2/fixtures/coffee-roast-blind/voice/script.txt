A green coffee bean tastes like grass. Nothing like coffee at all.
Roasting is what changes it. Around 160 degrees, the sugars inside start to brown.
Then, at about 196 degrees, the bean cracks. Literally. Roasters call it first crack.
Ten more seconds and you get a light roast. Ninety more and it's dark.
The darker you go, the more the bean's own flavour disappears, and the more it just tastes of roast.
So when a bag says dark roast, it isn't telling you where the coffee came from. It's telling you how long it burned.
